Most weak prompts fail the same way: they describe the situation at length and leave the model to *guess* what output you want. Answer-first prompting flips that. You state the deliverable up front, precisely, then supply context.

## The shape

```text
[ROLE / TASK]      One line: what to do.
[OUTPUT CONTRACT]  Exactly what to return, and in what format.
[CONTEXT]          The material to work from.
[CONSTRAINTS]      What to avoid, edge cases, tone.
```

The order matters. The model conditions on everything, but a crisp output contract near the top pulls the whole distribution toward the response you actually want.

## Make the output contract unambiguous

"Summarise this" invites a paragraph of unknown length. Instead:

> Return **exactly three bullet points**, each under 15 words, describing the customer's problem. No preamble.

If you'll parse the result in code, ask for JSON and give the shape. We go deep on that in the next lesson, *Structured output & JSON*.

## Show, don't only tell

One or two examples of a good answer (few-shot) usually beats another paragraph of instructions. The model is a pattern-matcher — a concrete example is the most efficient pattern you can give it.

## Why this works

From [How LLMs actually work](/learn/ai-foundations/how-llms-work): the model predicts the most probable continuation of your prompt. An explicit output contract makes your desired answer the most probable thing to come next. You're not "asking nicely" — you're shaping the distribution.
