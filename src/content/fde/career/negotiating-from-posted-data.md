The strongest negotiating position is a number you can point to on the other side's own website. This page separates comp figures that were actually fetched from a posting or a paying-transparency requirement from figures that come from a blog aggregating other blogs, and shows how to use each correctly.

## What is actually posted

A set of postings researched for this path state a real, pay-transparency-mandated range. Treat these as the floor of your anchor, not the ceiling:

| Company and role | Posted base range |
|---|---|
| Anthropic FDE | $280,000-$320,000 |
| Retool Deployed AI Engineer (SF) | $216,660-$293,250 |
| Harvey Senior Full Stack SWE, Forward Deployed | $200,000-$260,000 |
| Databricks Sr FDE | $182,000-$250,208 |
| Hebbia FDE (multi-level) | $180,000-$300,000 plus equity |
| Scale AI FDE, GenAI | $179,400-$224,250 |
| Harvey Founding FDE | $165,000-$200,000 |
| Glean Founding FDE | $160,000-$270,000 plus equity |
| Vercel Senior FDE (SF base) | $137,000-$207,000 |
| Palantir FDSE (NYC) | $135,000-$200,000 plus RSUs and sign-on |

Read across that table and the pattern is clear: posted US base ranges cluster between $135,000 and $320,000, with Anthropic the highest stated floor and Palantir and Vercel the lowest. OpenAI, Sierra, Cognition, Snowflake, Shield AI, Baseten, C3.ai, and every India-located posting researched for this path state no figure at all on the fetched page.

## What the aggregators add, and where to stop trusting them

Levels.fyi, which aggregates self-reported offers rather than postings, is worth citing because its methodology is at least visible and it draws from real reported packages. For the "Forward Deployed Engineer" title across companies, it shows a median total comp of $206,073, with the 25th percentile at $175,000 and the 75th at $278,000. For Palantir's Forward Deployed Engineer specifically, it shows a wider range, $185,000 to $631,250, with a median in the high $200,000s, and one worked example package of $227,000 total comp built from $164,000 base, $57,400 in annual stock, and a $5,200 bonus. Use Levels.fyi as a sanity check against a specific offer, not as the number you open with; it tells you whether an offer is in a normal band for the title, not what a specific company will actually pay you.

Indeed's own aggregate, reported by Business Insider, puts the average base for FDE postings at $171,911, with a typical range of $170,000 to $200,000-plus, a noticeably lower band than the posted-range table above because it is pulling from a much broader and less senior set of postings under the same title.

One further tier down is Perspective AI's "1,200 FDEs" compensation report, which synthesises Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, Blind, and pay-transparency postings into bands by company tier: frontier labs mid-level $385,000-$510,000, senior $560,000-$785,000; Series B-plus startups mid-level $250,000-$340,000; Fortune 500 mid-level $190,000-$240,000. The report states its own methodology is a synthesis of secondary sources, and that framing should be taken at face value: these are indicative bands built by combining other people's estimates, not fetched figures. Use them, if at all, only to sense-check whether a number you have already been offered sounds unusually low for the company's tier, never as a figure to state out loud in a negotiation as if it were data the other side would recognise.

## How to negotiate with this

1. **Anchor to the posted range for the specific company, if one exists.** If Anthropic's posting states $280,000-$320,000, that is the number to discuss, not a blog's estimate of "$385,000-$510,000 for frontier labs," which conflates companies and seniority levels the specific role may not match.
2. **Use Levels.fyi to check where in the posted range you should be asking, not to invent a number outside it.** A posting's range exists because the company is legally required to publish one in most of the US locations covered here; the real internal band for your specific level is narrower than the full posted range, and Levels.fyi's percentile breakdown is the closest public proxy for where that narrower band sits.
3. **When no range is posted, ask for one before naming a number yourself.** Every India posting researched for this path, and several US ones (OpenAI, Sierra, Cognition), state nothing. In that situation, the standard advice, letting the other side anchor first, applies with extra force, because you have no public data to counter-anchor against even if you wanted to.
4. **For India roles specifically, negotiate against comparable India offers, not against a dollar figure from a US aggregator.** Secondary, own-data guides put India ranges anywhere from roughly ₹18-28 lakh at a junior startup to ₹90 lakh-₹1.5 crore at a senior level inside a global lab's India office, with one recruiter's figure around ₹40 lakh for an experienced FDE. One vendor's guide states international bands run "50-70% of US" figures, but that line, like the Perspective AI comp report, is a synthesis without a stated primary source. Do not repeat either figure in a negotiation as if it were confirmed; use them only to judge whether an actual offer in hand feels reasonable.
5. **State your ask as a range anchored to the company's own posted band, plus a specific reason.** "Given the posted range of $200,000-$260,000 and my experience running the discovery-to-adoption cycle end to end on two capstone deployments, I'm targeting the upper half of that band" is a stronger opening than a number pulled from an aggregator the other side has no reason to trust.

The discipline underneath all five points is the same one this path teaches for sourcing any claim: a number you can trace to a page the other side published is worth far more in a negotiation than a number synthesised from other people's estimates, no matter how large or precise the second number looks.
